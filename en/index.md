## Invited Speakers

- **[Dr. Mario Carneiro](https://digama0.github.io/)** (University of Cambridge)

  **Title:** Metamath Zero: Trust All the Way Down

  <details markdown="1">
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  When a theorem is "formally verified", what exactly are we being asked to believe? Underneath the tactics there is an elaborator, underneath that a kernel, and underneath the kernel a runtime, a compiler, an operating system, and a chip. This year has made the question concrete: more than twenty soundness bugs were found in the Lean kernel, several by AI systems generating adversarial proof terms directly, and one of them briefly "disproved" the Collatz conjecture. The de Bruijn criterion (a small, independent checker re-checks everything) is a good design principle, but it is also an empirical claim about one particular program, and it deserves to be examined.

  This talk presents a research programme built around that question. Lean4Lean is a reimplementation of Lean's kernel in Lean together with a formal specification of its type theory and an in-progress proof that the one meets the other; the proof has already found a kernel bug present since Lean 4.0. Metamath Zero (MM0) is the other end of the spectrum: a proof language designed so that the checker stays under 3,000 lines of C, has seven independent implementations, keeps the specification and the proof in separate files, and fixes no foundation of its own. Its goal theorem, stated and proved in MM0 itself, is that a particular x86-64 executable is a correct MM0 verifier, with the proof generated by a compiler for a separation-logic-typed systems language. I will show the languages and the browser tools, report on how far the bottom theorem has progressed, including how the x86 specification is tested against real hardware, and sketch what it would take to keep going below the instruction set, to the gates and the transistors.

  </details>
